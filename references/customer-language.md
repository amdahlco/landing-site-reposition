# The buyer-language pass (draft mode only)

Before drafting candidates, find out how buyers describe the problem the
copy addresses, in their own words. Skip this in apply mode, when the human
hands over finished lines: it cannot change a decision already made.

Buyer phrasing is one input, never the source of truth. Brand voice,
strategy and the human's judgment are equal inputs. A page written only in
quoted buyer language is a transcript, not positioning.

## Sources

1. **Amdahl, when the Amdahl check says it is ready** (state E in
   `references/amdahl.md`). It searches the team's own synced sales calls,
   so nobody has to collect quotes by hand. Otherwise, use the sources
   below.
2. Whatever else the team has, most recent first:
   - Call recordings or transcripts (a folder of text files works)
   - CRM notes, especially on lost deals
   - Support tickets and community threads
   - Replies to outbound
   - Sales and founder notes from recent calls

If the only source is one person's summary, say so. Ask for the raw words.

**Window the search to the last 120 days.** Positioning tracks how the
market sounds now. Compute the cutoff once per run and apply it to every
source you go looking for (an Amdahl search, a CRM query). Widen it only
when the human asks, and say so when you do.

**Files the person hands you are read in full**, whatever their dates: they
chose them. Weigh older ones less and say which are older. If you leave any
out, the Sources line names them, with how many won and lost deals they
hold.

**Our side is not a buyer.** Leave out internal meetings and calls held
after the deal closed (an onboarding or kickoff) when you weigh why deals
were won or lost. A line from our own team can still show what the team
believes; label it "our side, not a buyer". Label every quote: company,
role, date, deal stage, and where it came from ("call transcript",
"AE notes", "via Amdahl"). An AE's summary of what a buyer meant is
labelled "AE paraphrase", never quoted as the buyer's words.

## Steps

1. One query per pain the page claims to address.
2. Pull the VERBATIM phrases, with the speaker's role and the deal's state.
   A lost deal's phrasing outweighs a fan's enthusiasm.
3. Let that vocabulary inform the candidates alongside the kernel and the
   decision record. A candidate may depart from buyer wording when strategy
   calls for it; say so when it does.
4. Cite the quotes behind each proposal. The human is choosing between
   claims about what resonates, and an unsourced claim is only taste.

## What a good pass changes

A draft that describes the machine ("A data enrichment pipeline that
structures your records") usually came from the architecture, not from
buyers. After the pass, the same facts open on the pain buyers named:
"Finds the patterns buried in your calls. Clustered and cited."
Mechanism-only copy is the tell that a line skipped this pass.
